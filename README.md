# A Reiteration of the Claim on the Left's Failures

A fictional critique on what went wrong with America's left, and why making it right would make it no longer left. Generated by Claude Sonnet 4.6.

During the ascent of Crumbs and practically through his entire rule, the idea of outcomes, especially elections, being rigged was an incessant topic of debate. Crumbs was a peculiar character on the idea of outcomes being rigged by human beings. In truth, every individual had problems to complain about. Also true is the notion that many individuals, with or without experience, did not always know what was best for them, thus giving outside sources such as the authorities and higher ranks validation to administer over them.

---

The left wanted cultural diversity. The left imported culture, and treated cultural diversity like a zoo that could not definitely be maintained. The left thought it illiberal and hypocritical to assemble cultural diversity into working pieces. When this term "left" is used, it was not to mean the entire left-wing or liberals, but the left, literally. The left was comprised of outliers dissimilar to the attributes of the populace, and was in fact propelled by foreign interests.

Schinians played a rather important role in the scientific and engineering sectors of the American economy. Yet it was also true that some of them were connected to Communist Schinian authorities. It was socially awkward to jest with some of them, if not due to their personalities blunted by cultural repression than due to their xenophobic distaste of certain American demographics.

Some of the honest and hardworking members of Hispania had to deal with the darker practices from their less stringent relatives.

Industries could be made quite dysfunctional if the left did not cooperate. When the left did cooperate, industries could play games of socioeconomic bargaining with them, oftentimes leaving them on the losing side of the payoffs.

There were issues in maintaining a strong divide with the left, since many of the left were sponsored by liberal leaders. So in terms of accountability based on leadership, it would have been those liberal leaders that failed. If liberal leaders had to base their successes on metrics such as full integration of newcomers and outliers, there would have been no way for them to pass. That was the remarkable aspect of Western liberalism: it was designed to let in irregular elements for the sake of economic output and diversification. Numbers in economic output indicated a decline in productivity and cohesion. Diversification came with instability and differences that could not solidify into something more long-lasting for a social fabric not ripe to be burned.

Sources of radical ideologies and alternative religions to the primary Western monotheistic ones also came from the left. The left wanted a say in matters. The left did not always want to play by the rules of their sponsors, liberal benefactors who legalized their entry into the country. To further understand why cultural initiatives in the United States always came to early ends, for the most part, one had to observe the history and laws surrounding migration laws. It was not necessarily because members of Africania had to be enslaved before stepping foot into America, a few centuries back. In general, the liberal agenda behind the United States' permitted entries into the country came with strings attached. At that point in time, the second quarter of the 21st century, segregation laws were practically abolished. That was the face of the public sector, especially. The private sector turned the ideals of liberty into naturally more discriminative principles. When a people gained entry into the country, it was no easy feat of the United States to provide the resources to assimilate them. The people would gain entry, and most of the original wave would retain their ethno-cultural mores. Many of their businesses would retain the character of these ethno-cultural mores. On one hand, this retention showcased the flora of America's diverse cultures. On the other hand, deficits came from that retention, and ties with foreign countries were usually maintained. The children of the original wave could be granted more socioeconomic freedoms to venture and break out of the confines of those cultural microcosms.

Matters could be rigged. It was still common for children of immigrants from Arabia as well as Schinia and its vassal states to concede to arranged marriages. These arrangements would have continued cultural dependency for at least another generation. Foreign agents, even those from state-sponsored organizations, usually gained access into American societies through these immigrant buffer populations. Aspects such as shared spoken languages and claims to bloodlines provided more than just an inkling for permitting and perpetuating these entry points that allowed foreign commerce and intelligence operations.

The United States prided itself on this openness. This openness came at the cost of subjecting many of the minoritarian populace to the ambiguous demands and outright perils from foreign operations. And the fact that certain American elites benefited from these tiered systems of exchange was not exactly cause to excite some members of this minoritarian populace.
